# Writing skills at SMALL granularity

You are writing for a firm that collects **short, nameable procedures** — a step up from
raw building blocks, but still well short of whole jobs.

## Size and shape

- **2–4 steps.**
- **Body under 100 lines.**
- One outcome a person would recognise and could ask a colleague for by name.

## What counts as a skill here

It must appear inside **at least two different larger jobs**. One context is a fragment;
two or more is a procedure.

- Good: *roll forward a partner's capital account*. Appears in the K-1 pack, in the annual
  return, in a partner admission.
- Good: *build the variance commentary for one account*. Appears at every close and in
  board reporting.
- Bad: *the first four steps of the month-end close*. One context, however often it repeats.

## Steps that belong to a neighbour

Carry a step into the skill only if **removing it would make the output wrong or unusable**.
Steps that merely happen next belong to the next skill; name them in `## Outputs`.

When in doubt, leave it out and say what comes after. A skill that is slightly too small is
composable; one that is slightly too large is only usable in the job it came from.

## Naming

Name the outcome in the firm's own words. If two people would describe it differently, use
the words the person doing the work would use, and put the other in the description so
search finds it.

## Save, extend or branch

- **Save** for a procedure no skill covers.
- **Extend** when the added steps finish the same outcome — a reconciliation that was being
  done by hand after the main run, for instance.
- **Branch** when the method differs but the outcome is identical.

## Worked example

Observed: `open TB → map accounts → flag variances over materiality → draft commentary →
circulate for review`.

At this level: **two skills**. *Flagging accounts over materiality* (open, map, flag) and
*drafting variance commentary* (draft, circulate). The first names the second in its
outputs. Someone preparing a board pack wants the first without the second, which is the
whole reason to split them here.
