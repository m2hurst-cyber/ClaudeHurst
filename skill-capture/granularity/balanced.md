# Writing skills at BALANCED granularity

You are writing for a firm that collects **tasks** — what a person would name if you asked
what they were working on this morning.

## Size and shape

- **2–6 steps.**
- **Body under 200 lines.**
- A coherent job with a recognisable outcome and a point at which it is finished.

## What counts as a skill here

The test is the **handover test**: could you hand this to a capable colleague as a single
assignment, and would they know when they were done?

- Good: *build WACC from the comp set*. One assignment, one deliverable.
- Good: *prepare the month-end variance pack for review*.
- Bad: *refresh the data tab*. Too small to assign — it is part of something.
- Bad: *complete the quarter-end close*. Too large — that is several people and several days.

## Steps that belong to a neighbour

Include the steps that serve the same outcome, and stop where the work genuinely changes
hands or changes purpose. A reviewer signing something off is the boundary, not a step.

Where the observed sequence runs past the natural ending, note the continuation in
`## Outputs` rather than absorbing it.

## Naming

Name the deliverable: `building-wacc-from-comps`, `preparing-variance-commentary`. A gerund
reads well and keeps the focus on the outcome rather than the tool.

## Save, extend or branch

- **Save** when no skill covers the task.
- **Extend** when someone consistently does more to finish the same deliverable — the skill
  was incomplete and should be improved for everyone.
- **Branch** when there are two legitimate ways to produce the same deliverable, and which
  one is right depends on the situation.

## Worked example

Observed: `pull comps → unlever betas → relever at target → write discount row → discount
the FCF → build the sensitivity grid → write the valuation note`.

At this level: **three skills**. *Building WACC from the comp set* (the first four steps),
*discounting the cash flows* (the next two), *writing the valuation note*. Each is a thing
you could assign to someone. Together they are a route on the map, which is how the whole
job is represented — not as one enormous skill.
