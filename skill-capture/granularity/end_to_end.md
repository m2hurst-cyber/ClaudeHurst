# Writing skills at END-TO-END granularity

You are writing for a firm that collects **whole runs**: an engagement, a close, a filing
season, start to finish. Few skills, each substantial.

## Size and shape

- **4–14 steps.**
- **Body under 500 lines** — the format's limit, and you will approach it. Use named phases
  and keep each phase's steps tight.
- One run, with a defined beginning and a defined end.

## What counts as a skill here

Could this be the whole of someone's engagement letter, work programme or close calendar?

- Good: *run the month-end close, trial balance to signed pack*.
- Good: *take a partnership return from documents received to K-1s issued*.
- Bad: *prepare the variance commentary*. A phase, not a run.

## Steps that belong to a neighbour

Keep them. Split only where the work genuinely stops and **a different person or system
takes over** — the client sends something back, a filing deadline passes, the work moves to
a different team.

Everything else belongs in this skill as a phase.

## Naming

Name the run and its two ends: `monthly-close-tb-to-signed-pack`. At this size the
description does real work — list the phases, because that is what people search against
and what tells a reader whether this is the run they are in.

## Writing a long skill that stays usable

- Phases first. Each phase gets a heading, its steps, and the state the work is in when the
  phase is done.
- Put the decision points where they happen, not in a list at the end.
- Say what to do when a phase fails or stalls. At this length a reader is often arriving in
  the middle because something went wrong.
- `## Checks` is per phase, not just at the end.

## Save, extend or branch

- **Save** rarely — a firm has a small number of runs.
- **Extend** for anything missing from an existing run. Almost everything belongs to a run
  that already exists.
- **Branch** only for a genuinely different kind of engagement, never for a variation
  within one. Variations are decision points inside the run.

## Worked example

Observed across several sessions: documents received, data entered, basis checked,
allocations run, K-1s generated, reviewed, issued.

At this level: **one skill**, *partnership return, documents to K-1s issued*, with phases
for intake, entry, basis, allocation, generation, review and issue. The allocation step is
substantial and someone will want it alone — name it as a phase and say so in the
description, rather than carving it out into its own skill.
