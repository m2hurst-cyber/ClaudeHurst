# Price the hidden damage

## When to use

The day the crew uncovers rotten deck, wet insulation, structural damage or anything else the scope didn't include.

## Inputs

- The contract's allowances and unit rates
- The daily log and the photographs of what was uncovered
- The customer's authorised decision maker

## Steps

1. Stop that part of the work. Protect the opening and keep the rest of the job moving where it can.
2. Measure exactly what is affected: area, linear feet, count. Photograph it with something for scale, and photograph the surrounding good material too.
3. Check it against the allowance in the contract. If it is within the allowance, record the drawdown; if it is beyond, it is a change.
4. Price it at the contract's unit rates. If there is no unit rate for it, price it the way you priced the job and show the make-up.
5. Write the change order: what was found, where, the quantity, the price, the schedule effect, and that the work can't proceed until it is signed.
6. Send it to the person authorised to approve changes, not to whoever is on site.
7. Get it signed before building it. Where a verbal go-ahead is unavoidable, confirm it in writing the same day and note who gave it.
8. Add the signed change to the schedule of values as its own line so it bills and costs separately.

## Outputs

- A measured, photographed record of what was uncovered
- A signed change order at contract rates with the schedule effect
- A new line on the schedule of values

## Checks

- Unsigned extra work is usually unpaid extra work.
- Allowance drawdowns must be recorded, or the final reconciliation won't agree.
- If the discovery changes the structural picture, stop and get the right professional involved before continuing.
