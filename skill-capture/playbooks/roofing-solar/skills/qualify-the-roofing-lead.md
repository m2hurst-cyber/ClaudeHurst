# Qualify the lead and book the survey

## When to use

Every new enquiry, before a survey is booked. A survey costs a half day of someone's time; this step decides whether to spend it.

## Inputs

- The enquiry: who called, what they say is wrong, the address
- Your current backlog and crew availability
- The service area, trades and roof systems you actually take on

## Steps

1. Record the lead and its source (referral, repeat customer, general contractor, storm canvas, web, utility partner). Source tells you later which ones are worth paying for.
2. Identify the building: address, type (residential, commercial, industrial), roof system if known, storeys, approximate age.
3. Ask what happened and when: leak, storm, age, re-roof planned, solar wanted, failed inspection. Ask whether anyone has been on the roof already.
4. Find the decision: owner, property manager, general contractor, board, tenant. Ask who signs and who pays, and whether there are other bidders.
5. Find the money: cash, financing, insurance claim, capital budget, public funds. Each one changes the paperwork, so note it now.
6. Ask the timing: when they need it done, and anything that fixes the date (lease, sale, inspection, incentive deadline, tenant move-in).
7. Decide: bid, decline or refer. Say why. A decline is a result, not a failure, and it belongs in the record.
8. Book the survey with the access the site needs: ladder, lift, roof hatch key, escort, safety induction.

## Outputs

- A qualified lead with source, building, decision maker, funding and timing
- A bid / decline / refer decision with the reason
- A booked survey with the access arranged

## Checks

- Don't book a survey without knowing who decides and how it is paid for.
- If it is an insurance claim, say so now: the scope comes from the carrier and the branch for it is different work.
- Note whether a competitor is already bidding. It changes how much estimating time is worth spending.
