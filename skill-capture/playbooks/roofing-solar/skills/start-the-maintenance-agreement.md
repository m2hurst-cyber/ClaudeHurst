# Start the maintenance agreement

## When to use

At closeout, when the customer takes an inspection, maintenance or monitoring agreement, or when a manufacturer warranty requires periodic inspection.

## Inputs

- The closeout packet and the warranty conditions
- Your standard maintenance scope and pricing
- The service calendar

## Steps

1. Write the scope: how many visits a year, what each one covers, what is reported, what is excluded, and what emergency response the customer is buying.
2. Price it for the real work: travel, the visit, the report, minor repairs included, and the rate for anything beyond.
3. Set the term, the renewal and the notice period, and say how price changes work.
4. Schedule the visits in the service calendar now, with the season that suits the work (and the manufacturer's required interval where one exists).
5. Set the invoicing to match: annual in advance, or per visit.
6. Record what the warranty depends on, so an inspection that is skipped shows up as a risk rather than a surprise at claim time.
7. For solar, set the monitoring review: what counts as underperformance, who looks, and how often.
8. Tell the customer what they get and when, and give them one number to call.

## Outputs

- A signed maintenance or monitoring agreement with scope, term and price
- Visits scheduled and invoicing set
- Warranty-critical inspections diarised

## Checks

- An agreement that never gets scheduled is a liability, not revenue.
- If the manufacturer's warranty needs an inspection at an interval, the agreement must cover that interval.
