# Collect the overdue balance

## When to use

When an invoice passes its due date, and at every month end across the ledger.

## Inputs

- The receivables aging and the invoices behind it
- The contract's payment terms and any pay-when-paid clause
- Your jurisdiction's notice and lien deadlines

## Steps

1. Age the balance by invoice, not by customer total. One old invoice inside a current account is the problem.
2. Check the job record before calling: was the application complete, were the waivers and payroll attached, is there an open punch item being used as a reason?
3. Check the deadlines that don't move: preliminary notice, notice of intent, lien filing, and the days each one is counted from. Diary them before the conversation, not after.
4. Send the statement: invoices, dates, amounts, what has been paid and the balance, with the documents attached again.
5. Call the person who actually releases payment, ask for a date, and write the date down.
6. Confirm the promise in writing the same day.
7. If the date passes, escalate one step: a letter, then the notice your jurisdiction provides for, then counsel. Each step on its own date, not when someone remembers.
8. Record every contact in the job file. Collection depends on a record that looks the same to a third party as it does to you.
9. Feed it back: a customer who always pays late should be priced or contracted differently next time.

## Outputs

- Aged balances with a named reason and a promised date for each
- Statements sent and promises recorded
- Notice and lien deadlines diarised

## Checks

- Lien rights are lost by calendar, not by argument. The deadline is the one thing that can't be recovered.
- Don't let a disputed punch item hold a whole application: bill the undisputed part and resolve the rest separately.

## Notes

Notice and lien deadlines, forms and wording are set by state law and vary widely. Confirm yours with counsel; this step is a prompt to check them, not a substitute.
