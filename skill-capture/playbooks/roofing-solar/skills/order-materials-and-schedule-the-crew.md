# Order the materials and schedule the crew

## When to use

After the permit, before the install window. Ordering early ties up cash; ordering late stops a crew.

## Inputs

- The takeoff with quantities and waste
- Supplier quotes and current lead times
- The crew calendar and the permit's constraints

## Steps

1. Convert the takeoff into purchase orders by supplier, with the job number on every line so the invoice lands on the right job.
2. Check lead times against the install window, especially for anything made to order: metal, curbs, skylights, membranes in a colour, inverters.
3. Schedule delivery to the site for the day it is needed, with where it goes and who receives it. Rooftop loading needs its own plan.
4. Book the dumpster (and the pulls), the lift or crane, and any traffic or parking permit.
5. Assign the crew by name and size, with the lead who is responsible on site.
6. Book the install window with weather in mind, and say what happens if it rains: how far you dry-in each day.
7. Send the pre-start note to the customer: dates, hours, access, noise, where the dumpster goes, where to move cars.
8. Hold the pre-job briefing: scope, hazards, tie-off plan, inspection points, what to do when something unexpected is uncovered.

## Outputs

- Purchase orders against the job number
- Confirmed deliveries, dumpster, equipment and crew
- A pre-start note sent and a briefing held

## Checks

- Never order against an estimate that hasn't become a contract.
- Every PO carries the job number, or job costing will be wrong at the end.
- If the material slips, move the crew before the crew arrives to no material.
