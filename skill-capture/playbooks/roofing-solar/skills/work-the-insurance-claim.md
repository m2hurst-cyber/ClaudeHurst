# Work the insurance claim

## When to use

When a storm, hail or wind claim is paying for the work. The scope comes from the carrier, which means your survey is evidence, not just an estimate.

## Inputs

- The carrier's scope of loss and estimate
- Your measurement report and photographs
- The applicable building code items for this jurisdiction

## Steps

1. Read the carrier's scope line by line against your own measurements. List every difference in quantity, material and labour.
2. Identify what the scope misses: accessories, flashing, drip edge, ventilation, underlayment, steep or high charges, disposal.
3. Identify code-required items the scope omits (ice and water barrier, decking requirements, ventilation standards) and cite the code section.
4. Photograph each disputed item so it stands on its own: damage, measurement in frame, overview and close-up.
5. Write the supplement: item, quantity, unit price, why it is required, and the evidence reference.
6. Submit it to the adjuster, log the date, and follow up on a schedule rather than waiting.
7. Record every conversation and decision in the job file, including what was approved and what was denied.
8. When the revised scope is agreed, reconcile it to your own estimate and tell the customer what they owe beyond the claim, including the deductible.
9. Only then write the contract, on the agreed scope.

## Outputs

- A line-by-line comparison of carrier scope to your measurements
- A supplement with evidence and code citations, submitted and tracked
- An agreed revised scope reconciled to your estimate

## Checks

- Never offer to cover, waive or rebate the customer's deductible. It is illegal in many states and it voids claims.
- The customer, not you, is the carrier's insured; keep them informed of what is being claimed in their name.
- If the carrier's price and your cost can't be reconciled, say so before signing, not during the tear-off.
