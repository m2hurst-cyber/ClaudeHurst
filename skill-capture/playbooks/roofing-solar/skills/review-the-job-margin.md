# Review the job margin and report the month

## When to use

When a job closes, and again at month end across all jobs.

## Inputs

- Final job cost by code against the budget from the estimate
- The contract values, billings to date and costs to date for open jobs
- Receivables aging and the backlog of signed, unstarted work

## Steps

1. Compare actual to estimate by cost code, not just in total. A job that lands on the number with labour over and material under got lucky, not good.
2. Explain each variance over your threshold in one line: what happened, and whether the estimate or the execution caused it.
3. Update work in progress: cost to date, estimated cost to complete, percentage complete, earned revenue, and over- or under-billing per job.
4. Flag jobs billed well ahead of cost, and jobs costing ahead of billing. The second kind is where cash disappears.
5. Update the backlog: signed work not yet started, by month, against crew capacity.
6. Age the receivables and name the slow payers, with what is being done about each.
7. Feed the estimating rates back: production rates per square, waste actually used, crew days per job type.
8. Build the month's report: margin by job and by type, backlog, collections, safety, and the three things that need a decision.

## Outputs

- Variance by cost code with reasons
- Updated WIP, backlog and aging
- A month report that ends in decisions, not just numbers

## Checks

- WIP only means something if cost to complete is honest. Ask the person running the job, not the spreadsheet.
- Chronic over-billing hides a margin problem until the last period, when it can't be hidden any more.
- Estimating rates that are never corrected from actuals will drift until a season of jobs is priced wrong.
