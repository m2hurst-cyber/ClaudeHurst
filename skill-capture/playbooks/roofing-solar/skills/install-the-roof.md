# Install the roof and keep the daily record

## When to use

Every day the crew is on site. The daily log is what progress billing, change orders and any later dispute all rest on.

## Inputs

- The scope, drawings and the permit's inspection points
- The daily log sheet and the photo index
- The contract's unit rates for extras

## Steps

1. Log the day before you leave site: crew names, hours, weather, what was installed and how much.
2. Record quantities in the same units as the takeoff (squares, linear feet, each), so percentage complete means something.
3. Dry-in to a defensible point each day. Note what is temporarily protected and what is exposed overnight.
4. Photograph progress in the same fixed order as the survey, plus anything unexpected before you cover it.
5. When something is uncovered that isn't in the scope, stop that part, photograph it, measure it and raise it the same day. That is the hidden-damage branch.
6. Keep the safety record: toolbox talk, tie-off, fall protection inspection, anyone new on site.
7. Call the inspections the permit requires at the stage it requires them, and don't cover inspected work before it is signed off.
8. Keep the site clean daily: magnet sweep, debris, protection of landscaping and the occupied space below.
9. Tell the customer when the schedule changes, the same day it changes.

## Outputs

- A daily log with crew, hours, weather and quantities
- A photo record tied to the plane and the date
- Field changes raised the day they are found
- Safety and inspection records

## Checks

- Quantities in the log must reconcile to the takeoff units, or the pay application won't tie.
- No cover-up before a required inspection.
- Extra work without a signed change order is work you will probably not be paid for.
