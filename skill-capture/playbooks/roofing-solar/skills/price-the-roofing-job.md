# Price the job

## When to use

After the survey, for every job you intend to bid. Use the same workbook each time so jobs can be compared afterwards.

## Inputs

- The measurement and condition report
- The estimate and takeoff workbook (resource)
- Current material prices, your burdened labour rates and your overhead rate

## Steps

1. Convert measured area to squares by plane, then apply a waste factor that suits the cut-up: simple gable, hips and valleys, or complex.
2. Take off materials line by line: covering, underlayment, ice and water, insulation, fasteners, flashing, edge metal, ridge, sealant, drains. Price each at today's cost, not last quarter's.
3. Add labour by task (tear-off, deck repair allowance, dry-in, install, detail work, clean-up) in crew hours, then convert to crew days at your real production rate.
4. Use burdened labour: wages plus payroll taxes, insurance, workers' compensation and benefits. An unburdened rate is how a job looks profitable and isn't.
5. Price equipment and logistics: lift, crane, dumpsters and the number of pulls, delivery, fuel, parking or lane closure permits.
6. Price subcontracted scope from quotes, not memory. Note what each quote excludes.
7. Add job-specific costs: permit fees, engineering, testing, temporary protection, night or weekend premiums, occupied-building constraints.
8. Apply overhead at your rate, then the margin the job deserves: risk, access, season, customer and how hard the schedule is.
9. Sanity-check the price per square (or per watt for solar) against your recent jobs of the same type. If it is far off, find out why before sending it.
10. Write down the allowances and exclusions the price depends on; they become the proposal's scope.

## Outputs

- A takeoff priced by cost code: material, labour, equipment, subcontractor
- Overhead, margin and the sell price, with price per square
- Allowances, exclusions and unit rates for extras

## Checks

- Margin is not markup. Decide which one the workbook is applying and label it.
- Every allowance in the price must appear in the proposal as an allowance.
- If a material quote is older than its validity, re-quote it or say the price holds only to a date.
