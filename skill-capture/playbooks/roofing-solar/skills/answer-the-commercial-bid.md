# Answer the commercial bid

## When to use

When the customer issues a package rather than accepting a proposal: public work, a general contractor's invitation, a property manager's RFP.

## Inputs

- The bid package: instructions, specification sections, drawings, bid form, addenda
- The commercial bid response checklist (resource)
- Your bonding capacity, insurance limits and reference list

## Steps

1. Read the instructions to bidders first, and write down the hard facts: due date and time, delivery method, format, number of copies, whether late is accepted (it isn't).
2. List every required deliverable as its own line: bid form, bid bond or security, insurance certificate, licence, references, safety record, schedule, submittal register, signed addenda.
3. Diary the questions deadline and the pre-bid walk. Attend the walk if it is mandatory; a bid from a no-show is usually rejected.
4. Read the specification sections that apply to your scope, then read the drawings against them, and list every conflict as a question.
5. Submit questions in writing before the deadline; rely on the written answer in the addendum, not on what anyone said at the walk.
6. Price the base bid, then each alternate and each unit price separately, on the same cost basis.
7. Check the commercial terms you are accepting by bidding: retainage, payment days, liquidated damages, prevailing wage, bonding, warranty term, schedule.
8. Fill the bid form exactly as written: every blank, words and figures matching, signed and sealed as required, addenda acknowledged.
9. Assemble, check against the deliverable list a second time with another person, and submit early enough to fix a problem.
10. Record what you bid, against whom if you learn it, and the result. A lost bid with a known price is worth more than a won bid with no record.

## Outputs

- A complete, on-time bid with every required document
- Alternates and unit prices on the same basis as the base bid
- A bid record for later comparison

## Checks

- A missing bid bond or an unacknowledged addendum gets the whole bid thrown out, however good the price is.
- Don't qualify or except your way around the specification unless the instructions allow it; many packages reject a qualified bid.
- Know the commercial terms before you bid. They are not negotiable afterwards.
