# Permit the job

## When to use

After contracting, for any work your jurisdiction requires a permit for. Solar adds an electrical permit and a utility application; that is the solar branch.

## Inputs

- The signed contract and the scope
- Drawings, product data sheets and any engineering the authority asks for
- Your licence, insurance certificates and contractor registration

## Steps

1. Confirm what this authority requires for this scope and building: building permit, electrical, structural letter, historic or HOA approval, dumpster or lane permit.
2. Assemble the application: forms, scope description, product approvals, fastening pattern, uplift or engineering where required.
3. File it, pay the fee, record the application number and the date, and ask the counter what the review time usually is.
4. Track the review. Answer corrections quickly and keep each answer with the job; a plan-check comment is a scope change in disguise more often than it looks.
5. When the permit is issued, record its number, expiry, posted conditions and every inspection it requires, with the stage each one happens at.
6. Tell operations what the permit constrains: working hours, access, dumpster placement, required inspections before cover-up.
7. Diary the expiry. A permit that lapses mid-job stops the job.

## Outputs

- An issued permit recorded with number, conditions and expiry
- The inspection schedule the install has to work around
- Plan-check answers kept with the job

## Checks

- Don't start work that the permit requires to be inspected before cover-up.
- If plan check changes the scope, price it as a change order before building it.
- Keep the permit posted on site if the authority requires it.
