# Contract the job and open it

## When to use

When a proposal is accepted, before any material is ordered or any crew is booked.

## Inputs

- The accepted proposal and the priced estimate behind it
- Your contract form, or the customer's for review
- The job numbering and cost code scheme you already use

## Steps

1. Issue the contract on the accepted scope and price. If the customer issues theirs, read it against your proposal and reconcile every difference in writing.
2. Check the commercial terms you have to live with: payment days, retainage percentage and release, liquidated damages, backcharges, insurance limits, indemnity, termination, dispute venue.
3. Write the change-order process into the contract: who can authorise, at what unit rates, and that work stops until a change is signed.
4. Record the payment schedule: deposit, milestones or monthly progress billing, retainage, final payment.
5. Note the statutory steps your jurisdiction expects at the start of a job (preliminary notices, notice of commencement, right-to-cancel periods for residential work). Diary them; several of them have deadlines measured from the first day on site.
6. Open the job: number, name, address, contract value, start and finish dates, estimated cost by code, and the margin you expect.
7. Copy the estimate into the budget by cost code, so actuals land against something later.
8. Hand over to operations: scope, drawings, allowances, site constraints and the customer's contact.

## Outputs

- A signed contract with scope, price, payment schedule and change process
- An open job with number, budget by cost code and expected margin
- Diarised notice and cancellation deadlines

## Checks

- Don't order material against a verbal acceptance.
- If the customer's form contradicts the proposal, the contradiction has to be resolved before signing, not noted.
- The budget in the job file must tie to the estimate that won the work.
