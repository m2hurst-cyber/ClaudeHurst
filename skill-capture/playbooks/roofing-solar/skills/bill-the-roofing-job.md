# Bill the job

## When to use

At each billing cycle on a progress-billed job, at the milestones on a fixed-schedule job, and at final completion.

## Inputs

- The schedule of values and pay application workbook (resource)
- The daily log quantities and the signed change orders
- The contract's retainage percentage and payment terms

## Steps

1. Update the schedule of values: scheduled value by line, work completed previously, work completed this period, stored materials on site.
2. Base percentage complete on measured quantities from the daily log, not on a feeling. Say which it is when they differ.
3. Add every signed change order as its own line with its own scheduled value. Unsigned changes are not billed; they are chased.
4. Compute retainage at the contract rate, the total earned less retainage, less previous payments, to the balance due this period.
5. Attach what the customer's process needs: photographs, stored-material evidence, certified payroll on public work, updated schedule.
6. Prepare the lien waivers the payment requires. Conditional on progress payment, unconditional once a payment clears; the forms and the wording are set by your state, so use the right one.
7. Collect the same waivers from subcontractors and suppliers for their part of the payment, before you release it.
8. Send the application to the right person in the right format, on the day of the month their process expects, and record when it went.
9. Record the payment when it arrives, against the invoice, and update the job and the aging.

## Outputs

- A pay application that ties to the contract, the changes and the log
- Lien waivers given and collected
- Updated receivables and job cost

## Checks

- Earned to date minus retainage minus previous payments has to equal the balance due. Check the arithmetic every period.
- Never sign an unconditional waiver for money you haven't received.
- Billing more than you have built is a fast way to lose the retainage argument at the end.
