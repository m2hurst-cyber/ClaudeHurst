# Write the proposal

## When to use

After pricing, for any job where you write your own proposal rather than filling in someone else's bid form. For an RFP or bid package, use the commercial bid branch as well.

## Inputs

- The priced estimate with its allowances and exclusions
- The proposal and scope letter template (resource)
- The warranty terms you offer on this system, and the manufacturer's

## Steps

1. Describe the work in the customer's language: what comes off, what goes on, which areas are covered, how the building is protected.
2. State the system by name: manufacturer, product, thickness or weight, colour, fastening or attachment method.
3. List inclusions plainly, then exclusions just as plainly: deck replacement beyond the allowance, structural work, asbestos, interior repairs, HVAC disconnection, landscaping.
4. Give the allowances with their unit rates, so a change costs what the contract says rather than what gets argued later.
5. Set the schedule: start window, working days, weather dependency, and what the customer must do (clear access, move vehicles, keep pets in).
6. Set the payment terms: deposit if any, progress payments against milestones, retainage, final payment on completion, and what triggers each.
7. State both warranties: your workmanship term, and the manufacturer's product or system warranty with what it requires (certified installer, inspection, registration).
8. Say how long the price holds, and sign it yourself.
9. Send it, log the date, and set the follow-up before you close the file.

## Outputs

- A signed-ready proposal with scope, exclusions, allowances, schedule, terms and warranties
- A follow-up date in the record

## Checks

- An exclusion the customer learns about on tear-off day is a dispute. Write it down now.
- Don't promise a manufacturer warranty the job doesn't qualify for: check the certification and inspection conditions first.
- If the price depends on doing the work in one mobilisation, say so.
