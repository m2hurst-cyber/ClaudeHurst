# Manage the subcontractors

## When to use

Whenever a crane, electrician, sheet metal shop, framer or any other sub works on your job.

## Inputs

- The scope being subcontracted and its budget from the estimate
- Your subcontract form and insurance requirements
- The lien waiver forms your state uses

## Steps

1. Scope the subcontract tightly: what is included, what is not, who supplies material, who cleans up, whose equipment, and the schedule they must hold.
2. Issue the subcontract with the price, payment terms (including retainage and when it is released), insurance requirements and indemnity.
3. Collect the certificate of insurance before they arrive, with the limits you require and your company named as it must be. Diary the expiry.
4. Confirm the licence and registration the work requires, and the safety expectations: tie-off, inductions, who is responsible on site.
5. Brief them with the same job information your crew gets: access, hours, hazards, inspection points, what to do when something unexpected appears.
6. Record their work in the daily log like your own: who was there, what they did, how much.
7. Check each invoice against the subcontract and the work actually done, and hold back retainage at the agreed rate.
8. Collect the conditional waiver with the invoice and the unconditional waiver once their payment clears, before you bill that work onward.
9. Keep their certificates, waivers and sign-offs in the job jacket; your closeout and your own warranty may depend on them.

## Outputs

- Signed subcontracts with scope, price and terms
- Current insurance certificates on file before work starts
- Sub invoices checked, retainage held, waivers collected

## Checks

- No certificate of insurance, no start. The exception always becomes the claim.
- Paying a sub without their waiver leaves you exposed to a lien on your customer's building.
- A sub's change is still a change: it needs a signed change order before it is built, same as yours.
