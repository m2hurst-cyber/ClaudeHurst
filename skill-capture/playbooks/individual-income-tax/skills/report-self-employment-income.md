# Report self-employment income (Schedule C)

## When to use

Branch after "Report income" when the client answered yes to self-employment. One Schedule C per business.

## Inputs

- 1099-NEC and 1099-K forms, the client's own income records, and bank or bookkeeping reports
- Expense records, vehicle mileage log, home office measurements, asset purchases

## Steps

1. Total gross receipts from the client's records, then reconcile to the 1099-NEC and 1099-K forms (a 1099 is not the whole story, and 1099-K can include personal transfers).
2. Classify expenses into the Schedule C categories; ask for support for anything large or unusual.
3. Vehicle: standard mileage or actual expenses, from a contemporaneous log.
4. Home office: simplified method or Form 8829, only for space used regularly and exclusively for the business.
5. Assets bought: Form 4562 (depreciation, Section 179 or bonus, under this year's rules).
6. Compute net profit, self-employment tax (Schedule SE) and the deduction for half of it; consider the qualified business income deduction and self-employed retirement and health insurance deductions.
7. Rejoin the mainline: carry the profit to income and the estimated tax need to next year's plan.

## Outputs

- Schedule C, Schedule SE and supporting forms, with a reconciliation of receipts to 1099s

## Checks

- Receipts reconcile to the 1099s, or the difference is explained.
- A loss year, or losses several years running, is reviewed for hobby-loss risk.
- Next year's estimated payments account for self-employment tax.
