You will be asked one of three things. The difference matters, because each creates a
different kind of record.

## Save

*You repeated a workflow no skill covers.* Accepting proposes a brand new skill. It starts
in the sandbox with you as its contributor.

## Extend

*You finish an existing skill and then keep doing the same extra steps.* Accepting proposes
the **next version** of that skill, with your steps added. Everyone who uses it gets your
improvement — which is why extend has a higher bar than save.

## Branch

*You start and finish an existing skill but do the middle differently.* Accepting proposes
a **variant** that lives alongside the original. Both stay published; the map shows them as
alternatives. Branch when your way is genuinely different, not when the original is simply
out of date — that is an extend.

## Saying no

Three answers, all useful:

- **Not now** snoozes it. It comes back if the pattern keeps happening.
- **No** dismisses it permanently. That exact pattern will never be raised with you again.
- **Accept** sends a draft to your area's reviewers, with you named on it.

Dismissals are not wasted. They are the clearest signal that the system is cutting work at
the wrong size, and the granularity setting is how that gets corrected.

## Why you were not asked

A pattern has to repeat enough times, be long enough, and — at finer settings — show up in
more than one context before anyone is prompted. The judgement step then asks whether it is
a real task with a recognisable outcome rather than incidental clicking. Most things you do
twice will never become a prompt, deliberately.
