Nothing is published by being written. A skill climbs three stages, and each one is a
different promise about how much you can rely on it.

| Stage | Who sees it | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sandbox | Your area, its contributors, admins | Someone's way of doing it. Use with judgement. |
| Firm | Everyone in the firm, and agents | Reviewed, used, and voted for. The house way. |
| Market | Beyond the firm | Proven at firm level, licensed to share, with written agent directions. |

## The gates

Promotion is not a vote of confidence, it is a checklist that either passes or does not:

**To firm** — says what it does and when to use it; has clear steps; says what it produces;
has actually been used in the sandbox by enough people; and has net positive support.

**To market** — has held at firm level long enough; strong internal support; runs without
trouble at a good traffic tier; is licensed for sharing; and has written directions for the
agents that will run it.

## Who decides

A reviewer for the skill's area, who did not write it and did not request the promotion.
Market promotions need an admin. Only an admin can waive a failed gate, and only with a
written reason that is kept.

## If it is sent back

You get the reason. Fix the draft and request again — the history of both attempts stays,
which is the point: a skill that took three goes to pass is a more trustworthy skill than
one nobody looked at.
